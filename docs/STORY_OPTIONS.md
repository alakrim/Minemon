# Minemon: three storyline options

The owner picks one. Each option uses what the game already has: Sanctuaries ("the only places the night will not enter"), Dread creatures and Dread Shards, the Umbral Ball, 3 lives then permadeath as a ghost, real-time capture, coins, and the eldritch giants. Each also defines what M3 (dread), M4 (towns and missions) and M5 (giants) build, so the story and the systems grow together and nothing is bolted on.

Shared rules for all three:
- **Two players, one story.** The owner and the friend share the world's story state. Each has their own lives. A dead player stays in the story as a ghost with a role of their own.
- **The night is the antagonist's hand.** Every option explains why the dark is dangerous, why Sanctuaries are safe, and what Dread is.
- **Towns are earned, not just found.** In each option, missions change the town physically: lights come back on, NPCs move in, a shop opens. Progress is visible in the world.
- **Kanto, then Johto.** Johto opens in the second half. The eldritch giants are the region bosses, not random spawns.

## Option A: "The Long Night"
*Cosmic horror. The sun is dying, one night at a time.*

**Logline.** Each night lasts a little longer than the last. Something under the Ruins of Alph is drinking the daylight, and the old Lamplighter order that built the Sanctuaries is gone. You are the last people who can still carry a flame.

**Premise.** Three thousand years ago, the Unown were not letters but locks. They sealed away **the Umbra**, a hunger from outside the world that feeds on light and fear. The Lamplighters kept the seal with a network of shrines (our Sanctuaries), all fed by the sacred fire of the Bell Tower in Ecruteak. Ho-Oh carried the dawn across the sky; Lugia kept the deep currents that held the seal under the sea. Two hundred years ago, the Brass Tower burned. Ho-Oh left, and the seal has been failing ever since. Now it is breaking.

**What the systems mean in this story**
| System | In the story |
|---|---|
| Night | The Umbra's tide. A world **Eclipse clock** makes each night 10–20 s longer over time. Sealing an Anchor pushes it back. |
| Sanctuaries | Lamplighter shrines. Their lanterns are the last of the sacred fire. Relighting dead shrines is the main mission type. |
| Dread creatures | Creatures the Umbra has touched. Their eyes are open holes, and the Unown script crawls on their skin. |
| Dread Shards | Pieces of the Umbra itself: powerful, and dangerous to carry. A rumoured mechanic: carrying many makes the night notice you. |
| Umbral Ball | Lamplighter craft: a cage of shadow to hold shadow. |
| Giants | **Anchors**: huge creatures the Umbra has fused with and rooted into the land (a Gyarados coiled through Cerulean Cave, an Onix stretched under Rock Tunnel, a Dragonite circling Mt. Silver's peak). Kill or cleanse each Anchor and the clock goes back. |
| 3 lives | The Lamplighters' "three wicks". At death, a wick burns out at the nearest shrine. |
| Ghost (permadeath) | You join the Lamplighters' ghosts. A ghost can see Umbra trails the living cannot, and can guide the other player. |

**Structure**
- **Act I: Ember (Kanto, M3–M4).** You wake at the last lit shrine near Pallet. Professor Oak is missing, and his notes talk of "letters that move". You relight the Kanto shrines one by one: Viridian, Pewter, Cerulean, and so on. Each Gym Leader guards a shrine and has their own reason to have let it go dark. Lavender Tower is where the dead whisper which shrine falls next. Act ending: Silph Co.'s roof is the highest light in Kanto, and you see the dark sweeping in from the west, from Johto.
- **Act II: Bell (Johto).** You cross into Johto. The Ruins of Alph are open, and the Unown are rewriting themselves. Morty in Ecruteak is the last real Lamplighter, half-blind from looking at the Umbra too long. The legendary beasts Raikou, Entei and Suicune are the burned tower's three guardians. You must find each one, and each is half Dread. Capture or cleanse decides how they help you later.
- **Act III: Dawn.** You climb the Tin Tower to call Ho-Oh back with the sacred fire you have carried from every shrine. Meanwhile, under the Whirl Islands, Lugia holds the seal shut with the last of its strength. Final set piece: the Umbra's heart rises under the Ruins of Alph. One player must hold the light at the Tin Tower while the other goes down into the Ruins, so the co-op is forced and on purpose.

**Horror beats.** The Unown script appears on walls you built yourself. Sanctuary lanterns flicker when an Anchor moves. A "dawn" comes, and it is fake.

**Endings**
- **Dawn:** Ho-Oh returns and the clock resets. Dread still exists, but only on the darkest nights (the endgame grind).
- **Eclipse:** you fail, or choose to keep the Umbra's power. Permanent night, and you become the new Lamplighters, ruling a dark world from the shrines.
- **Secret:** catch Celebi at the Ilex Shrine and go back to the night the Brass Tower burned.

**Why pick it:** it's the most horror, it gives the strongest single mystery, and it uses the systems we already have most directly (night, Sanctuaries, Dread). **Risk:** the Eclipse clock is a world-wide mechanic that needs careful tuning (the admin panel helps).

## Option B: "Rocket's Harvest"
*Conspiracy and techno-horror. The monsters were made.*

**Logline.** Pokémon across Kanto are turning on people. Silph Co. says it's a virus. It isn't. Team Rocket found a way to harvest fear, and you have been helping them without knowing it.

**Premise.** Under Cinnabar, the Mewtwo project found that a terrified Pokémon releases something they named **Dread**. It crystallises, and it's a near-perfect energy source. Rocket, now a polished company front called **Umbral Industries** under Silph, needs Pokémon terrified on purpose. Their signal towers broadcast a frequency that makes wild Pokémon aggressive at night, and makes some of them Dread. The Sanctuaries? Pre-Rocket radio silence zones, built by the old Pokémon League, which the signal can't reach.

**What the systems mean in this story**
| System | In the story |
|---|---|
| Night | Signal hours: the towers broadcast from dusk to dawn. |
| Sanctuaries | Old League bunkers, shielded from the signal. Fixing their shields is the main mission type. |
| Dread creatures | Signal victims: in pain, not evil. Capturing one is a rescue. |
| Dread Shards | Rocket's product. **Moral economy:** a Rocket fence in Celadon pays very well for shards, but every sale powers the next tower, and the nights in that zone get worse. |
| Umbral Ball | Rocket technology that you steal and copy. Its first blueprint is a mission reward. |
| Giants | **Rocket weapons**: Pokémon forced into a huge size by shard implants (a towering Nidoking with a city on its back, a Muk the size of a district, Mewtwo's failed clones). Each has a control collar that a stealth mission can sabotage before the fight. |
| 3 lives | Medical: Sanctuary keepers have three bio-stabilisers per trainer. |
| Ghost | A ghost can slip through Rocket walls and read terminals: the other player's spy. |

**Structure**
- **Act I: Outbreak (Kanto).** Towns are locked down. Each one has a Rocket problem in disguise: Pewter's museum is smuggling fossils for implants, Cerulean's water carries shard dust, and Lavender's ghosts are Pokémon who died in the labs. Gym Leaders are split: some are on Rocket's payroll, and you find out which by playing.
- **Act II: Signal (Johto).** The Radio Tower in Goldenrod is the master transmitter. Undercover missions: put on a Rocket uniform (an item), walk the lab floors, and don't get noticed. Your friend's choices in Kanto (which leaders they exposed) change who helps you here.
- **Act III: Source.** Cinnabar's deepest lab: Mewtwo is the original Dread source, kept in permanent terror for twenty years. The final choice: free it (Mewtwo destroys Rocket, and maybe you), or catch it with a Master Ball that Rocket built for exactly this (Rocket loses, but you now own the thing they hurt).

**Endings:** freedom, control, or profit. The profit ending: you sold enough shards to buy Umbral Industries, and the night is now yours to sell.

**Why pick it:** it has the most interconnected choices (the shard economy changes the world, and the Gym Leader alignments change Act II), human villains with clear motives, and stealth missions that vary the gameplay. **Risk:** it's less pure horror, and the stealth plus faction tracking adds new systems (uniform, detection, reputation).

## Option C: "Ashes of Kanto"
*Post-apocalyptic survival mystery. The world already ended. You are rebuilding it.*

**Logline.** Ten years ago, every trainer in Kanto vanished in one night: the Quiet. Their Pokémon went wild. Towns emptied. You wake in a Sanctuary, the first new trainer in a decade, and the creatures remember what humans did to them.

**Premise.** Nobody knows what happened. Pallet is grass and foundations. The survivors hide in the Sanctuaries, which are the last places the creatures won't attack. That is where you find your starter: the last one Oak raised, waiting in its ball for ten years. The truth, revealed slowly: Red, the champion, went up Mt. Silver to challenge the one thing he had never beaten. There he found what the giants are: **old Pokémon that have outlived their trainers**, grown huge and mad with grief. On the night of the Quiet, the oldest of them, a creature older than the League itself, took every trainer it could reach to "keep them". They are not dead. They are somewhere.

**What the systems mean in this story**
| System | In the story |
|---|---|
| Night | When grieving Pokémon hunt people, trying to take them as the Quiet did. |
| Sanctuaries | Shelters. The survivor communities live here. |
| Dread creatures | Pokémon who belonged to vanished trainers. Their grief has turned them; some still wear their trainer's belongings. **Capturing one gives you a "Lost Trainer's Memory"**, a piece of the mystery. |
| Dread Shards | Crystallised grief. Used to build towns. |
| Umbral Ball | Made by survivors to bring lost Pokémon home. |
| Giants | **The Abandoned**: ancient Pokémon in every region, each tied to one vanished Gym Leader (Brock's Onix, grown into a mountain range; Misty's Starmie, a second moon over Cerulean). Beating one frees its trainer. |
| 3 lives | Bluntly mortal. No mystic reason: medicine is scarce. |
| Ghost | The Quiet's ghosts: you see the other side and the trapped trainers. |

**Structure (settlement building plus mystery)**
- **Act I: Survivors.** Rebuild Pallet, then Viridian, then Pewter. Each town is a building project: missions to bring back a healer, a smith (shop items) and a farmer (food). NPCs physically move in. Rebuilt towns are where new Sanctuaries go, and they make the night safer around them, so the map turns from dark to lit as you play. Recovered Memories reveal the Quiet in pieces (non-linear, like Outer Wilds).
- **Act II: The Abandoned.** Each Kanto Gym Leader is freed by beating their giant. Brock comes back first, aged ten years in one night. Freed leaders start their Gyms again, which gives the badge structure a reason in the story.
- **Act III: Silver.** Johto was spared. It is a living, normal region across the mountains, which is a shock after Kanto. But the giants are moving towards it. The climb to Mt. Silver: Red is up there, the last one still holding the oldest giant back.

**Endings:** bring everyone home (all leaders freed, Red saved); take Red's place (the hold continues, and you stay on the mountain as the next champion); or the Quiet again (fail, and Johto empties too).

**Why pick it:** it fits Minecraft's building best (your towns are your progress), it has the most emotional story, and the co-op is natural (one builds, one scouts). **Risk:** settlement building is the largest new system (NPC moving, building templates), and it is the least "Pokémon journey" in feel.

## Side by side
| | A: The Long Night | B: Rocket's Harvest | C: Ashes of Kanto |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tone | Cosmic horror, myth | Conspiracy, techno-horror | Grief, survival, hope |
| Horror level | ★★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★ |
| Uses current systems | Most directly | Reframes them | Reframes them |
| New systems needed | Eclipse clock, shrine relighting | Shard economy, factions, stealth | Town rebuilding, NPC settlers, memories |
| Build cost (M4–M5) | Medium | Medium-high | High |
| Co-op design | Forced split finale | Spy ghost, shared choices | Builder and scout roles |
| Replay | 3 endings, Celebi secret | 3 endings, branching Act II | 3 endings, order of Memories |

**My recommendation:** **A, "The Long Night"**. It's the most horror, the most mysterious, and the cheapest to build, because the night, Sanctuaries and Dread already *are* its core. Ideas from the others can be folded in: B's shard trade (sell shards and the night gets worse nearby) and C's lit-town map (relit shrines push the dark back) both fit A without changing its story.
